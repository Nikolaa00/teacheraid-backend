TeacherAid v1 Specification

The build specification for TeacherAid version one, reorganized into seven categories so that a non-technical reader can find every decision, every rule and every gap.

Source dated 15 September 2026Organized 17 September 2026Catalogued items —Empty cases —

About the source

Everything in this document comes from one file: feature-spec-v1.md, kept in the TeacherAid second brain at Areas/product/. Its own header describes it as created on 15 September 2026, type "area", status "draft — not yet integrated into the vault". No author is named in the file.

The specification says of itself that it is "written for the developer who will build it", that it "says what exists, how it behaves, and what happens when it fails", and that where it disagrees with an older product note, the specification is right.§1

How this document was made

  • One source only. Nothing was taken from the older notes the specification refers to (features, product-overview, user-flows, technical-architecture, data-and-privacy, the handbook). Where the specification is silent, the gap is marked, not filled.
  • No invention. Two categories — user stories and acceptance criteria — contain only material the specification itself frames that way. It frames none, so both are recorded as empty cases with pointers to the nearest material.
  • Every statement is traceable. A reference such as §5.4 points to the numbered section of the source file. Items carry stable identifiers (FR-12, BR-7, EC-3, UF-8, E-21) so they can be cited in meetings, tickets and contracts.
  • Wording follows the source. Sentences are rewritten for a general reader, but the facts, numbers and exclusions are the specification's own. Macedonian terms stay in Cyrillic where they are the real name of a thing.

Category 1 of 7

Product and business context

What TeacherAid v1 is, where it is meant to be used, what is deliberately left out, and the unverified assumptions the whole specification rests on.

What the product is

Composed only from the specification's own sections; each clause cites where it comes from.

A teacher prepares a lesson in advance by picking a unit from the pre-loaded national e-учебник. The system generates an opener, five to eight pop-up questions with answers and explanations, a suggested timer for each, and a review summary — in Macedonian, all editable.§4.1

In class, the teacher runs the lesson from a laptop with two browser windows: a class view on the projector and a private console on the laptop screen.§5.2 Students join on their own phones by scanning a QR code that changes every 30 seconds, signed in with their school email account.§5.4 A focus lock — a native phone app that keeps distracting apps closed — is mandatory; without it a student cannot join.§6.1

The lesson opens with a prediction or a debate: students vote, then write one line of reasoning, and earn points for the reason whether it was right or wrong.§5.5 During teaching, the teacher fires pop-up questions with timers at moments of their choosing.§5.6 A student can send the teacher a question that classmates never see attributed.§5.7 Points, badges and per-semester leaderboards run throughout.§13

When the teacher presses End, points settle, attendance is written, the list of absent students appears formatted for copying into e-Дневник, and the most-missed questions are reviewed.§5.10, §8.1, §8.2 Afterwards the teacher can send an optional practice set assembled from that class's own missed questions,§8.3 and sees attendance, points and per-topic results per student on a dashboard.§8.4, §9.1 A school admin manages classes and rosters and sees which teachers are using it;§9.2 the headmaster receives a monthly PDF report by email and never logs in.§9.3

Scope of v1

CTX-1

Where. Secondary schools in North Macedonia — гимназија, стручно and уметничко.§2

CTX-2

Pilot subjects. History, electronics and physics. These three have their curriculum and e-учебници loaded properly. Any other subject works from the teacher's own input, with no curriculum grounding.§2

Business reasoning the specification records

The specification explains why several features exist in business terms. Those explanations are collected here; the features themselves are on later slides.

CTX-10

The school library (every teacher's saved lessons visible to colleagues in the same school) is "what makes a whole-staff licence pay off immediately: one keen teacher's work seeds the building instead of fifty-five people each paying the same setup cost alone." It is also "the first safe step toward the national corpus named as the moat" in the competitors note.§4.7

CTX-11

One lesson, many classes is "what makes the ~400 generations per teacher per year in the cost-structure note real rather than aspirational", and lets a teacher compare how two classes did on the same question.§4.5

CTX-12

The nudge (a quiet console suggestion when no question has been fired for several minutes) protects the teacher who got absorbed in teaching — "the failure mode that produces an empty dashboard and a school that looks inactive".§5.6

CTX-13

The admin's usage overview is "the early warning for a school where only the champion teacher is active", which "somebody inside the building needs to see before renewal month, not after".§9.2

CTX-14

The headmaster report is split into a forwardable page one and a headmaster-only page two because "a school ranked ninth of twelve will not forward a page that says so", and in a twelve-school pilot most schools are outside the top few.§9.3

CTX-15

The staff-access log is "the most convincing single thing to show a headmaster on the data question".§11.4

CTX-16

The absentee list is "the smallest feature in the spec and probably the moment a teacher first feels the product saved them something".§8.1

CTX-17

Legitimacy with МОН (through cleared e-учебник rights) "is the main defence against Curipod" — the only competitor the specification names.§4.1

CTX-18

Onboarding at scale. The guided first lesson "has to work fifty-five times in one week without a founder in the room, because the whole staff arrives at once rather than one volunteer at a time".§10.2

CTX-19

The retention metric the specification cites is lessons-per-teacher, with intervention below two per week; because practical lessons are excluded, it should be measured per theory period.§2

Blocking legal item

CTX-20

The national e-учебници are published under МОН and written by named authors. Pre-loading them centrally into a commercial product "is a rights question that must be resolved with МОН/БРО before this feature ships — alongside the ДПА and the privacy notice already budgeted for a lawyer". The specification calls this "not a launch-and-see item" and "the kind of thing that becomes a headline if discovered later".§4.1

Category 3 of 7

User flows

The step-by-step sequences the specification describes: who starts each one, what triggers it, the steps in the order given, and what it produces. Only sequences the specification actually spells out are included.

Preparing a lesson

UF-1

Creating a lesson from the e-учебник

Who: teacher · Source: §4.1

This is the primary way a lesson is made. The specification describes it as "two taps" plus an optional sentence.

  1. The teacher picks the subject, the year and the unit from the pre-loaded e-учебник contents.
  2. Optionally, the teacher adds one sentence of emphasis — the specification's examples are "focus on the exam question" and "they struggled with this last year".
  3. Generation returns the opener, 5–8 pop-up questions with answers and explanations, a suggested timer per question, and the review summary. All of it is in Macedonian and all of it is editable.
  4. The teacher reviews, edits inline or applies a one-tap refinement (UF-3), and saves.

Result: a saved lesson — a reusable object that can be run many times (§4) and is visible to colleagues in the same school through the library (UF-4).

TeacherAid pre-loads and chunks the e-учебници for the three pilot subjects, per unit. Teachers do not upload them.§4.1

UF-2

Creating a lesson without a loaded book — the fallback

Who: teacher · Source: §4.1

"Type three sentences" is now the fallback path, used when there is no loaded book for that subject, or when the teacher wants something off-book. The specification says it "still works" and is "no longer the headline". No further steps are described.

UF-3

Refining a generated lesson

Who: teacher · Source: §4.6

On the generated lesson there is a fixed row of one-tap actions. The teacher taps one:

  • Easier
  • Harder
  • More questions
  • Make the opener a debate (or a prediction)
  • Shorter timers

There is no free-text chat. These actions cover the structural changes that inline editing cannot do — "turning a prediction opener into a debate is not an edit to a field".

UF-4

Reusing a colleague's lesson from the school library

Who: teacher · Source: §4.7
  1. A teacher sees a lesson another teacher in the same school has saved.
  2. They copy it into their own account.
  3. They edit their copy. The original is never modified by a copy.

No national library, no cross-school sharing, no public lessons in v1.

UF-5

Running one lesson for several classes

Who: teacher · Source: §4.5

The teacher runs the same lesson for II-1, II-2 and II-3. Each run is its own session with its own attendance, answers, points and results. Editing the lesson between runs affects later runs, not the record of earlier ones.

The live session

UF-6

Setting up the room: two windows

Who: teacher · Source: §5.2

The teacher runs the lesson from a laptop with two browser windows. The class view is projected; the console stays on the laptop screen and is never projected. The class view must be openable as a separate window with one click, "not by dragging" — setting up an extended display with thirty students waiting is named as a real friction point, and onboarding (UF-18) must cover it explicitly.

UF-7

The session, state by state

Who: teacher advances · Source: §5.3
lobby→opener→teaching→review→ended

The teacher advances the state. The server is the timer authority for every countdown; the phones and the class view only render it.

UF-8

A student joins

Who: student · Source: §5.4, §3.2, §6.1

Preconditions stated elsewhere: the student is enrolled in the class (§3.2) and has authorized focus lock (§6.1).

  1. The class view shows a QR code and a short code. The QR rotates every 30 seconds.
  2. The student scans it.
  3. The student is authenticated by their school-email session.
  4. The student joins and is marked present, with a timestamp. A late join is timestamped as late.

Attendance here is TeacherAid's own. Official attendance stays in e-Дневник (§8.1).

UF-9

The opener — prediction (the default)

Who: teacher and students · Source: §5.5
  1. A "What will happen if…?" question with 3–4 options appears.
  2. Students vote.
  3. Students type one line of reasoning.
  4. Live bars show on the class view.
  5. The reveal.

Points for being right; points for giving a reason, right or wrong. Reasons are shown to the class without names; the teacher's console shows names.

UF-10

The opener — debate

Who: teacher and students · Source: §5.5
  1. A claim is shown.
  2. Students pick a side and give one reason.
  3. The teacher reads two reasons aloud and lets the class argue.
UF-11

Firing a pop-up question

Who: teacher · Source: §5.6
  1. The teacher taps once on the console.
  2. The question lands on every phone with its timer running.

Pop-ups are teacher-triggered, never scheduled — "unpredictability is the point". The teacher can also fire an ad-hoc question ("quick: yes or no?") without preparation. If several minutes pass with no question fired, the console shows a quiet suggestion — not a modal, not a sound.

UF-12

Skipping a bad question

Who: teacher · Source: §5.8
  1. The teacher skips the question during the session.
  2. It is removed from the session and nobody is scored on it.
  3. The class view says so plainly, so students know it does not count.

There is no live text editing. The teacher fixes the lesson afterwards, in the copy they reuse.

UF-13

A student asks a question the class will not see attributed

Who: student, then teacher · Source: §5.7
  1. At any time during the lesson, the student sends a question. The limit is 3 per student per lesson by default.
  2. It arrives in the inbox on the teacher's console, with the sender's name. The inbox lives on the console only.
  3. The teacher answers it in the review block (UF-14).

No classmate ever sees who sent it.

UF-14

The review block

Who: teacher · Source: §8.2

The class view shows the most-missed questions, ranked worst first, and the anonymous questions for the teacher to answer aloud. The teacher supplies the explanation — no AI re-explanation, no generated remedial content; "nothing unreviewed reaches students".

UF-15

Ending the session

Who: teacher · Source: §5.10, §8.1
  1. The teacher presses End. There is no auto-close.
  2. Points settle.
  3. Attendance is written.
  4. The absentee list appears: only the names of students who were absent, formatted for copying into e-Дневник.
  5. The lesson is archived.

The teacher retypes the absent names into e-Дневник — "four names instead of checking thirty". TeacherAid does not write to e-Дневник and does not replace it.

After the lesson

UF-16

Sending a practice set

Who: teacher, then students · Source: §8.3
  1. After a lesson, the teacher sends an optional practice set to the class.
  2. The set is auto-assembled from that session's own missed questions — no new generation, no cost, no unreviewed content.
  3. Students do it voluntarily; it scores points.
  4. The teacher sees who completed it and how they scored.

Onboarding

UF-17

The class setup lesson

Who: a whole class, with the teacher present · Source: §10.1

Because focus lock is mandatory and needs a native app plus an operating-system permission, each class spends one period on setup before its first real lesson:

  1. Install the app.
  2. Sign in with the school email.
  3. Authorize the lock — with the teacher present "to fix the five phones that resist".

Forty-five minutes given up once per class. The specification marks this as an unverified assumption (C, §14).

UF-18

A teacher's first run — the guided first lesson

Who: teacher · Source: §10.2

Not a video. The app walks the teacher through building one real lesson end to end:

  1. Pick the unit from the e-учебник.
  2. Review the questions.
  3. Run it.

They finish holding a lesson ready for tomorrow. The walkthrough must cover opening the class view as a second window — "the step most likely to go wrong in front of a class".

Administration and reporting

UF-19

The monthly headmaster report

Who: automatic · Source: §9.3
  1. Monthly and automatically, a PDF is sent to the headmaster by email. No login, nothing to configure.
  2. Page one is written to be forwarded without editing; page two is for the headmaster only (contents in FR-59).
  3. The email carries a permanent private link to every past report — one unguessable URL, no account.

The school admin can resend the report (§3.3).

UF-20

Roster changes

Who: school admin · Source: §12
  • Add a student mid-year. They start at zero points; the class leaderboard must show they joined partway through.
  • Remove a student. Their data is retained under the retention rule (§11.6) until deletion is requested.
  • Move a student between classes. Their history moves with them.
UF-21

TeacherAid staff accessing a school's data

Who: TeacherAid support · Source: §11.4
  1. A support incident occurs. There is no standing access.
  2. Access is granted for that incident, time-limited.
  3. It expires automatically.
  4. The access is written to an audit log, and the school admin can see every occurrence.
UF-22

A request to export or delete a student's data

Who: the school, then TeacherAid support · Source: §11.5
  1. The school makes a written request.
  2. Support manually exports everything held on that one student, or deletes it.

No self-serve export or deletion exists for the admin in v1.

Situations the specification does not cover

E-45Empty case · answering after the timer

What happens to an answer submitted after a question's timer has run out is not stated.

E-46Empty case · reaching the question limit

What a student sees or can do after sending their third anonymous question in a lesson is not stated (§5.7).

E-47Empty case · joining the wrong session

A student can only join a session they are enrolled in (§3.2). What happens when a student scans the code of another class's session is not described.

E-48Empty case · focus lock revoked mid-session

The specification states that a student without authorization cannot join (§6.1). What happens if a student revokes authorization after joining is not stated.

E-49Empty case · the teacher's laptop goes offline

Phone dropouts (§5.9) and total failure (§5.9) are covered. The case where only the teacher's laptop loses connection while the phones are fine is not.

E-50Empty case · the class view is lost mid-session

What happens if the projected window is closed or the projector fails during a session is not stated (§5.2).

E-51Empty case · two open sessions

Whether one teacher can have two sessions open at once — for example after a forgotten End (§5.10) — is not stated.

E-52Empty case · generation fails or returns too little

Generation returns 5–8 questions (§4.1). What happens if it fails, or returns fewer, is not stated.

E-53Empty case · a missing or wrong e-учебник unit

What the teacher does when the pre-loaded unit they need is missing, or its content is wrong, is not stated (§4.1).

E-54Empty case · a student's school email changes

The email is the student's identity (§3.2). What happens when a school changes or reissues it, or two students share one, is not stated.

E-55Empty case · more than 35 students

The 35-student session assumption is named (§2). What happens in a larger class is not stated.

E-56Empty case · joining late during the opener

Whether a student who joins after the opener has started can still vote and give a reason is not stated (§5.4, §5.5).

E-57Empty case · numeric answer format

A numeric question accepts a typed number within a tolerance (§4.2). How decimal commas, units or spaces are handled is not stated.

Category 7 of 7

Acceptance criteria

The conditions under which each feature counts as done — statements that can be checked and passed or failed before a school is handed the product.

Status: empty

E-58Empty case · acceptance criteria

The specification contains no acceptance criteria. No statement in it is framed as a condition of acceptance, a test, or a "done when". By the rule chosen for this document, none have been derived.

E-59Empty case · definition of done

There is no definition of done for any requirement, area or release.

E-60Empty case · pilot exit criteria

The specification names a pilot repeatedly (§2, §9.3, §14) but sets no conditions for a pilot to be judged successful or ended.

What the specification offers that is adjacent

These are kept where they are in this document, not restated as criteria, because the specification presents them as behaviour or as open items — not as tests.

The measurable values above are the natural starting points; the qualities stated without a measure are where a decision is needed before a criterion can be written.

Glossary

Plain-language expansions for the reader. The specification uses these terms without expanding them; the expansions below are general knowledge, not taken from the specification.

e-учебник / e-учебници
The national electronic textbook(s) for a subject, published under МОН and written by named authors.
e-Дневник
The official electronic class register in which attendance is recorded. TeacherAid never writes to it.
МОН
The Ministry of Education and Science of North Macedonia.
БРО
The Bureau for Development of Education, the ministry's curriculum body.
ДПИ
The State Education Inspectorate.
ЗЗЛП
The Law on Personal Data Protection (2020), North Macedonia's GDPR-aligned data law.
ДПА
The data processing agreement between a school (the data controller) and TeacherAid (the processor).
гимназија / стручно / уметничко
The three kinds of secondary school: general (grammar), vocational, and arts.
практична / лабораториска настава
Practical or laboratory lessons, as opposed to theory periods.
Focus lock
The specification's term for the native phone app feature that keeps distracting apps closed during a lesson.
Opener
The prediction or debate question that starts every lesson.
Pop-up
A timed question the teacher fires to every phone during teaching.
Class view / console
The two windows of a live session: the projected one and the teacher's private one.
Session
One run of a lesson with one class. The same lesson can be run as many sessions.
Hypercorrection, productive failure
Learning-research effects the specification cites as the reason for scoring a written reason whether right or wrong.
